For most retirees, Social Security is the one source of income that is guaranteed for life and adjusts for inflation. That makes the decision of *when* to claim one of the most important in your whole retirement plan. Once you start, you generally can't change your mind.

Here are five questions we walk through with every client before they file.

## 1. What is my full retirement age?

Your full retirement age (FRA) is when you receive 100% of the benefit you've earned. For anyone born in 1960 or later, it is **67**.

You can start as early as 62, but your check is permanently reduced, by up to 30%. You can also wait past FRA. For every year you delay, up to age 70, your benefit grows by 8%.

## 2. How long do I expect to need the income?

Nobody knows exactly how long they will live, but family history and your own health give useful clues. If you expect a long retirement, delaying often pays off, because the larger check keeps coming for decades. If health is a concern, claiming earlier may make more sense.

## 3. Am I married, and how will my choice affect my spouse?

Married couples have more choices, and more at stake:

- **Spousal benefits** can be worth up to 50% of your spouse's benefit at full retirement age.
- **Survivor benefits** mean that when one spouse dies, the survivor keeps the larger of the two checks.

That second point is often overlooked. If the higher earner delays, they are also protecting their spouse's income for life.

## 4. Will I still be working?

If you claim before full retirement age and keep working, Social Security may temporarily withhold part of your benefit once your earnings pass an annual limit. Those dollars aren't lost forever; your benefit is recalculated later. Even so, the withholding can catch people by surprise.

## 5. How will taxes affect my benefit?

Depending on your other income, up to 85% of your Social Security benefit may be taxable. The order in which you draw from IRAs, 401(k)s and other savings can change how much of your benefit is taxed. Planning your withdrawals together with your claiming date can make a real difference.

## The bottom line

There is no single "right" age to claim. The best choice depends on your health, your marriage, your other savings and your plans. A Social Security review is a standard part of every retirement income plan we build.
