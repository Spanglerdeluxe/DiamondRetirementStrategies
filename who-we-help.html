Planning for your stage of life.

Retirement looks different at 55 than at 75, and different for a couple than for someone on their own. Here is how we help the people we serve most often.

The people we serve

Six situations we know well.

Most of our clients are between 55 and 80 and live in Phoenix and the surrounding Valley communities. Many come to us at a turning point.

5 to 10 years out

Pre-retirees

You have saved well and want to know if it is enough, and how to turn it into a paycheck.

  • Retirement date and income projections
  • Social Security claiming strategy
  • 401(k) and pension decisions
  • Protecting savings from a late market drop

Already retired

Retirees

You want your money to last as long as you do, with fewer surprises along the way.

  • Income that keeps pace with your spending
  • Required minimum distributions
  • Annual Medicare review
  • Long-term care protection

Planning together

Married couples

Two retirements, two Social Security records and one shared future to plan for.

  • Coordinated claiming for both spouses
  • Survivor income planning
  • Pension payout options
  • Beneficiary and legacy review

Life changes

Widowed & divorced

A loss or a divorce can mean making big financial decisions alone, often for the first time.

  • Survivor and divorced-spouse benefits
  • Inherited IRA rules
  • Re-titling accounts and policies
  • A calm, unhurried pace

Public service

Federal employees & educators

TSP, FERS, 403(b) and state pension plans come with their own rules and deadlines.

  • Thrift Savings Plan options
  • 403(b) and 457 planning
  • Pension and survivor elections
  • Health coverage after service

Building to exit

Business owners

Your business may be your largest asset. Your retirement depends on how you leave it.

  • Business succession planning
  • Buy-sell agreement funding
  • Owner retirement plans
  • Key-person protection

For adult children

Helping a parent plan?

Many families first contact us on behalf of a mother or father. We welcome that.

With your parent's permission, we are happy to include you in meetings, explain options in plain language and help organize documents. Common topics include:

  • Long-term care costs and how to pay for them without draining savings
  • Medicare and Medicaid enrollment and eligibility
  • Beneficiaries and estate documents so wishes are clear
  • Income after the loss of a spouse
