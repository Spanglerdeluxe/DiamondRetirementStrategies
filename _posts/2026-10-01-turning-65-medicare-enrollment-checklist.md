Medicare is one of the biggest milestones of retirement, and it comes with deadlines that don't move. Getting the timing right protects you from gaps in coverage and from late-enrollment penalties that can last as long as you have Medicare.

## Know your seven-month window

Your **Initial Enrollment Period** is seven months long. It starts three months before the month you turn 65, includes your birthday month, and ends three months after.

Signing up early in the window means your coverage can start on the first day of your birthday month.

## Are you still working?

If you or your spouse are still working and covered by an employer's group health plan, you may be able to delay Part B without a penalty. The rules depend on the size of the employer, so check before you decide.

When that job or coverage ends, you generally have an eight-month **Special Enrollment Period** to sign up for Part B.

One more detail: if you contribute to a Health Savings Account (HSA), those contributions must stop once you enroll in Medicare.

## Understand the penalties

- **Part B:** your premium can go up 10% for each full 12-month period you could have had Part B but didn't sign up.
- **Part D (drug coverage):** a penalty applies if you go 63 days or more without creditable drug coverage after your initial window.

Both penalties are usually permanent.

## Choose your path

Once you're enrolled in Original Medicare (Parts A and B), you have two broad choices:

1. **Original Medicare plus a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan and a Part D drug plan.** This offers wide freedom to see any doctor who accepts Medicare.
2. **A Medicare Advantage plan (Part C)**, which bundles coverage through a private insurer, often with a provider network.

## Don't miss your Medigap window

Your **Medigap Open Enrollment Period** lasts six months and starts the month you are both 65 or older and enrolled in Part B. During that time, insurers generally can't turn you down or charge you more because of your health. After it ends, you may have to answer health questions to buy a supplement.

## Your checklist

- Mark your 65th birthday month and count back three months
- Decide whether employer coverage lets you delay Part B
- Stop HSA contributions before Medicare starts
- Compare Medigap and Medicare Advantage options
- Choose drug coverage so you avoid the Part D penalty
- Review your plan every fall during Open Enrollment (October 15 – December 7)
