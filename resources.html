Mark your calendar

The birthdays that change your retirement.

Many retirement decisions are tied to a specific age. Missing one can cost you penalties or benefits.

AgeWhat happensWhy it matters
50Catch-up contributions beginYou can put extra money into 401(k), 403(b), TSP and IRA accounts each year.
55The "rule of 55"If you leave your job in or after the year you turn 55, you may withdraw from that employer's 401(k) without the 10% penalty.
59½Penalty-free withdrawalsYou can take money from IRAs and most retirement plans without the 10% early-withdrawal penalty.
60–63Larger "super" catch-upWorkplace plans allow a higher catch-up limit in these four years.
62Earliest Social SecurityBenefits start, but are permanently reduced by up to 30% compared with waiting until full retirement age.
65Medicare eligibilityYour 7-month enrollment window starts 3 months before your 65th birthday month. Late enrollment can mean lifelong penalties.
67Full retirement ageFor anyone born in 1960 or later, you receive 100% of your Social Security benefit.
70Maximum Social SecurityYour benefit grows 8% for each year you wait past full retirement age, up to age 70.
70½Qualified charitable distributionsYou can give directly from an IRA to charity and keep the gift out of your taxable income.
73Required minimum distributionsYou must start withdrawing from tax-deferred accounts. The age rises to 75 for those born in 1960 or later.

Based on federal rules as of 2026. Rules change and your situation may differ; please confirm with us or your tax professional before acting.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions.

Don't see your question? Call us at 480.750.2999.

Does the first meeting cost anything?

No. Your first consultation is free and carries no obligation. It is a chance to ask questions and see whether we are a good fit.

How are you paid?

As licensed insurance professionals, we are typically compensated by the insurance company when you choose to put a policy or annuity in place. There is no charge for our planning meetings, and we will always tell you how we are paid on any recommendation.

Do I need a certain amount saved to work with you?

No. We work with people at many levels of savings. A good plan matters just as much with modest savings as with large ones.

What is a fixed indexed annuity?

It is a contract with an insurance company that protects your principal from market losses while allowing interest to be credited based on a market index, up to a limit. Many can provide guaranteed lifetime income. They are long-term products with surrender periods, so they are not right for every dollar or every person. We explain the details before you decide.

I'm already retired. Is it too late to plan?

Not at all. Many of our clients are already retired. We can review your income, Medicare coverage, required distributions and beneficiaries, and look for ways to make your savings last longer.

Can you work with my CPA or attorney?

Yes. With your permission we coordinate with your tax professional and attorney so everyone is working from the same plan.

Do you only work with people in Phoenix?

Most clients live in Phoenix and across the Valley, and we are happy to meet in person. We can also meet by phone or video if you live elsewhere in Arizona.

Plain English

Retirement terms, explained.

Annuity
A contract with an insurance company that can turn savings into a stream of income, now or in the future.
Beneficiary designation
The form that names who receives an account or policy when you die. It overrides instructions in your will.
Full retirement age (FRA)
The age when you receive your full Social Security benefit. It is 67 for anyone born in 1960 or later.
Medicare supplement (Medigap)
Private insurance that helps pay costs Original Medicare does not cover, such as deductibles and coinsurance.
Required minimum distribution (RMD)
The amount you must withdraw each year from tax-deferred accounts once you reach the required age.
Rollover
Moving money from one retirement account to another, such as a 401(k) to an IRA, without paying taxes on the move.
Roth conversion
Moving money from a traditional IRA to a Roth IRA. You pay tax now so future qualified withdrawals are tax-free.
Surrender period
The years after buying an annuity when withdrawing more than the allowed amount carries a charge.
Sequence of returns risk
The danger that a market drop early in retirement, while you are withdrawing, does lasting damage to your savings.
Long-term care
Help with daily activities like bathing or dressing, at home or in a facility, usually not covered by Medicare.
